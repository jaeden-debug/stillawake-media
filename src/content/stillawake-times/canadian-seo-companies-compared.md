**Canadian SEO companies differ on five structural things far more than on talent: how they price (monthly retainer, fixed project, or performance-based), how long they lock you in, who physically does the work, what "included" covers, and whether their reporting comes from Google Search Console or from their own rank tracker. Compare those five and the shortlist decides itself — the skill gap between competent firms is much smaller than the structural gap.**

This article is deliberately not a ranked list. Ranked lists of "best SEO company in Canada" are almost always paid placements or self-nominations, and we're not going to pretend ours would be different. What follows is the evaluation framework we'd want a prospective client to run on us.

## The one thing the search data tells you before you talk to anyone

In Google Keyword Planner (geo Canada, English, measured August 2026), `canadian seo companies` returns 2,400 searches/month at **low** competition, with a top-of-page bid range of **$11.88 to $62.97**. The broader term `seo optimization company` sits at 3,600/month. `best seo company canada` is only 480/month.

Two useful readings:

1. A five-fold spread within a single keyword means the market has no shared price for a click, which usually means it has no shared price for the service either. Expect quotes for the same brief to vary by 4x. They will.
2. If a firm buys that keyword at the top of the range, they are paying up to roughly $63 for one visitor who may or may not become a lead. At a typical 2–5% lead rate, customer acquisition runs into the thousands. That cost is inside your monthly fee. It's not a scandal — it's just a thing you should know when a firm's price seems high relative to the hours you can account for.

## The five axes

| Axis | Options in the Canadian market | The question that reveals it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pricing model | Monthly retainer, fixed-scope project, hybrid, performance/rev-share | "What exactly do I get if I pay you for one month and stop?" |
| Contract length | Month-to-month, 6-month, 12-month minimum | "What is your minimum term, and what happens in month 13?" |
| Who does the work | Named practitioner, in-house team, account manager + subcontractor | "What is the name of the person who will touch my site, and can I ask them a technical question before I sign?" |
| Scope of "included" | Technical only, technical + on-page, + content production, + links, + AEO | "How many pages of content are written per month, by whom, and chosen how?" |
| Reporting source | Google Search Console / GA4, or the agency's own rank tracker | "Will I get GSC clicks, impressions and position, or your rank tracker's numbers?" |

### Pricing model

**Retainer** is the default in Canada and it suits ongoing work: technical maintenance, content cadence, monitoring. Its failure mode is drift — month 9 looks like month 3 because there's no scope pressure. Guard against it by asking what the *plan* is for the next quarter, not the next month.

**Fixed-scope project** (an audit, a migration, a cleanup) is honest and easy to evaluate because there's a defined end state. Its failure mode is the deliverable nobody implements. If you buy an audit, budget for the implementation too, or buy them together.

**Performance-based** sounds like it aligns incentives. In practice it aligns them to whatever metric is in the contract. If the metric is "rankings", the firm will target keywords it can win rather than keywords that make you money. We don't offer it and we'd be sceptical of anyone who does, unless the metric is revenue and they have read-access to your commerce data.

### Contract length

A 12-month minimum is very common. The stated reason — "SEO takes time" — is true. The unstated reason is that it converts an uncertain client relationship into a booked year.

Our own position, for a concrete reference point: [published SEO plans](/pricing) at $600 and $850 CAD/month with **no 12-month contract**. We think the correct pressure points at the provider, not the client. That's a structural choice you can evaluate, not a claim about quality.

### Who does the work

This is the axis with the widest variance and the least disclosure. In a small Canadian shop, the person you meet is usually the person who works on your site. In larger firms, the sales lead, the account manager, and the practitioner are three different people, and the practitioner may be a subcontractor several time zones away.

Neither arrangement is disqualifying. Undisclosed subcontracting is. **The test: ask to speak with the person who will do the work, before you sign, and ask them one specific technical question about your site.** A firm that can't produce that person has told you something.

### What "included" means

Content production is the biggest hidden variable in every Canadian SEO quote we've seen. Two retainers at the same price can differ by four articles a month, which is most of the labour.

The second-biggest is language. In Québec, doing the work properly in English *and* French roughly doubles the content surface — and it's also the largest untapped opportunity in most markets here, because the French side is far less contested. (Our own keyword research, geo Québec, found Québécois write « site web » 2.6× more often than « site internet » — the kind of detail that decides whether French pages rank or sit unread.)

The third is whether AI-search work is in scope at all. [Answer engine optimization](/answer-engine-optimization) is now a real share of discovery, and many retainers written in 2023 don't mention it.

### Reporting source

Rank trackers report a position for a keyword in a simulated location. Search Console reports what actually happened: impressions, clicks, click-through rate, and average position for real queries from real users. Rank trackers are useful diagnostics; they are not results.

**Named failure mode:** the monthly report that leads with "17 keywords improved" and never mentions clicks. Ranking movement inside positions 30–60 changes nothing. Ask for clicks and impressions, from GSC, with a like-for-like period comparison.

## What a real result looks like when you strip the marketing

Two engagements we can describe with numbers rather than adjectives:

For [TravelDesign By Lisa](/work/lisa-travel-design), a client, the platform was 834 URLs across three languages with roughly 2,300 technical defects. Clearing those defects came first; the site went from zero organic clicks to page-one positions in eight weeks, with 9-minute average sessions and CLS of 0.00 (Chrome Lighthouse, mobile, August 2026). The lesson isn't the eight weeks — that was a favourable niche. It's the sequence: nothing else was going to work until the crawl and index layer did.

[Blackwater Aquatics](/work/blackwater-aquatics) is a business Jaeden founded and operates — not a client — which is why we can share the awkward numbers too. Its best product page runs an 8.61% click-through rate at an average position of 8.5, across roughly 60,000 impressions on its top-12 URLs (GSC, 180-day window ending 12 August 2026). Position 8.5 is page one, bottom half. It took months of education-led content before commercial pages moved at all.

## When hiring any SEO company is the wrong answer

Worth saying plainly, because most agency comparison content never does:

- **Your problem is conversion, not traffic.** If you already get relevant visitors and they don't contact you, SEO spend compounds the wrong problem.
- **You need a finite fix, not a program.** A migration, a redirect map, a schema implementation — buy the project, not the retainer.
- **You're pre-product-market-fit.** Ranking for a thing nobody wants is expensive.
- **Your site is 6 pages and your market is one town.** Do [local SEO](/local-seo) yourself first; the ceiling on effort is low and the payback is fast.

## A scorecard you can actually fill in

| Question | Good answer | Answer that should worry you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum term? | Month-to-month, or a project with an end date | 12 months, non-negotiable, with an early-exit fee |
| Who does the work? | A name, available before signing | "Our team" |
| Content per month? | A number, and who chooses the topics | "As needed" |
| Reporting? | GSC clicks/impressions/position, period over period | Rank-tracker screenshots |
| Price? | Published, or quoted from a written scope | Quoted only after they've sized your revenue |
| Guarantees? | None, explained honestly | Guaranteed page 1 |
