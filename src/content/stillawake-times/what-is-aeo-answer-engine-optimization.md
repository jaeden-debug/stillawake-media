# What Is AEO? Answer Engine Optimization, Explained

**AEO — Answer Engine Optimization — is the practice of structuring your content and business information so that answer engines select it when composing a direct answer.** An "answer engine" is any system that responds to a question with an answer instead of a list of links: Google's featured snippets and AI Overviews, voice assistants, and conversational AI like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude.

The one-sentence version: **SEO gets you ranked; AEO gets you quoted.**

## Why AEO exists now

Search behavior is splitting. A large share of questions that used to produce ten blue links now produce a composed answer — either at the top of Google (AI Overviews, featured snippets) or inside an AI assistant that never shows a results page at all. For those queries, the businesses that win aren't the ones ranked #1 through #10. They're the ones whose facts made it *into the answer*.

That changes what "optimized" means. A page can rank well and still be unquotable — buried answers, vague claims, pricing hidden behind a contact form. AEO is the discipline of fixing exactly that.

## AEO vs SEO: the practical differences

| | SEO | AEO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Optimizes for | Position in a ranked list | Selection into a composed answer |
| Unit of competition | The page | The passage, the fact |
| Content shape | Comprehensive coverage | Direct answers, extractable facts |
| Key question | "Is this page relevant and authoritative?" | "Can this be quoted confidently, out of context?" |
| Measured by | Rankings, clicks | Citations, snippet ownership, AI mentions |

They're complementary, not rivals — answer engines still draw heavily from content that ranks. The foundation is shared: crawlable structure, real expertise, technical health. AEO is the additional layer that makes that content *liftable*.

## What answer engines select for

Based on how these systems observably behave — and on running this playbook across our own properties and [client builds](/work):

**Direct answers, stated first.** The question, then the answer, in the opening sentences. This article's first paragraph is the pattern: a bolded, self-contained definition that survives being quoted with no surrounding context.

**Self-contained passages.** Every answer engine extracts fragments. A paragraph that depends on the three paragraphs before it can't be lifted. Write sections that stand alone.

**Extractable facts.** Prices, timelines, service areas, process steps — stated in plain text. We publish our SEO plans at $600–$850 CAD/month on our [pricing page](/pricing) partly for humans and partly because a stated price is a quotable price.

**Question-shaped structure.** Headings that match how people actually ask ("How much does…", "What is…", "How long does…"), each followed by an immediate answer.

**Verifiable specificity.** Engines are increasingly tuned to distrust superlatives. "We took a store's repeat-customer rate from 5.9% to 27.8%, measured in Shopify over a 30-day window" is selectable; "we deliver amazing results" is noise. Every number we publish carries its source and date in [our case studies](/work) for exactly this reason.

**Entity clarity.** The engine has to know *who is answering*. Consistent organization data, a real author with a real profile page, structured data that agrees across every page. Our articles resolve to a single author entity — [Jaeden Doody](/founder/jaeden-doody) — rather than four name variants, because scattered identity dilutes trust signals.

## What AEO is not

The term is young enough that a lot of nonsense travels under it. Things that are **not** AEO:

- **A guaranteed placement service.** Nobody controls what an answer engine selects. Anyone promising "we'll get you into ChatGPT" is overpromising by construction.
- **FAQ schema spam.** Adding FAQPage markup to invisible questions was already against Google's rules for snippets; it does nothing for AI systems either. (Worth knowing: Google restricted FAQ rich results to authoritative government and health sites back in 2023 — FAQ markup remains valid semantic structure, but it is not a rich-result play for a business site.)
- **Keyword stuffing with question phrases.** Engines select for answer quality, not question density.
- **A replacement for SEO.** Content that never gets crawled or trusted never gets quoted.

## How AEO connects to GEO

You'll also see the term **GEO — Generative Engine Optimization** — which focuses specifically on generative AI systems and the machine-readability layer underneath them (llms.txt, entity graphs, agentic navigability). ## A quick self-audit

Five questions that reveal most of the gap:

1. Does each commercial page answer its core question in the first paragraph — or does the answer arrive after eight paragraphs of throat-clearing?
2. Can a machine find your prices in plain text?
3. Would your key paragraphs make sense quoted alone, with attribution?
4. Do all your pages agree about your business name, location, and services?
5. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity the questions your customers ask. Are you in the answers? Are your *competitors*?

That last test is the whole game in miniature — it's free, takes ten minutes, and tells you where you stand today.
